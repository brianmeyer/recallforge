# Immunology and the Immune System

The immune system defends against pathogens through innate and adaptive responses. Innate immunity provides immediate, non-specific defense through barriers, phagocytes, and inflammation. Adaptive immunity develops specific memory for faster future responses.

Lymphocytes include B cells producing antibodies and T cells coordinating responses. Antigens trigger immune recognition. Vaccines train adaptive immunity without causing disease. Autoimmune diseases occur when immunity mistakenly attacks the body.

Immunotherapy treats cancer by activating anti-tumor responses. Monoclonal antibodies target specific proteins. CAR-T therapy engineers patient cells to attack malignancies. Transplant medicine manages immune rejection through immunosuppression while preventing infections.
