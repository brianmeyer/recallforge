# Impressionism Art Movement

Impressionism revolutionized painting in 1870s France. Artists including Monet, Renoir, and Degas rejected academic conventions for capturing fleeting light and atmosphere. They painted outdoors (en plein air) using visible brushstrokes.

Techniques included broken color, complementary contrasts, and pure unmixed pigments. Subjects were contemporary life: cafés, dancers, landscapes, and leisure activities. The movement challenged the Salon system through independent exhibitions.

Post-Impressionists like Van Gogh, Cézanne, and Gauguin extended the movement toward greater structure and symbolic content. Impressionism influenced photography, film, and subsequent modern art movements. Major collections reside in Musée d'Orsay and institutions worldwide.
