# Barbecue and Smoking Meat

Barbecue is low and slow cooking over indirect heat, typically 225-275°F for hours. This breaks down collagen into gelatin, creating tender, juicy meat with smoky bark exteriors.

Wood choice matters: hickory for strong flavor, apple for sweetness, mesquite for intensity. The smoke ring forms when nitrogen dioxide reacts with myoglobin in meat surfaces. It's a sign of proper smoking, not doneness.

Different regions have distinct styles. Texas favors beef brisket with salt and pepper. Carolina specializes in pulled pork with vinegar-based sauces. Kansas City uses tomato-based sweet sauces on ribs. Memphis dry-rubs ribs before smoking. Proper fire management maintains consistent temperatures throughout long cooks.
