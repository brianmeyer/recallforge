# Distance Running and Marathon Training

Marathon training builds aerobic endurance through progressive mileage increases over 16-20 weeks. Most plans include easy runs, tempo runs at lactate threshold pace, interval workouts, and long runs building to 20-22 miles.

Proper running form minimizes injury risk: slight forward lean, midfoot strike, cadence of 170-180 steps per minute, and relaxed shoulders. Common injuries include shin splints, plantar fasciitis, IT band syndrome, and runner's knee.

Race nutrition involves carbohydrate loading before the event and taking gels or sports drinks every 30-45 minutes during the marathon. The Boston Marathon requires qualifying times based on age and gender. Ultra-marathons extend beyond 26.2 miles, with popular distances of 50K, 50 miles, and 100 miles.
