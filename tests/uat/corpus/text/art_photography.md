# Photography as Fine Art

Photography evolved from scientific documentation to recognized art form. Early pioneers like Daguerre and Fox Talbot developed chemical processes. Pictorialists manipulated images to resemble paintings. Straight photography celebrated the medium's unique capabilities.

Technical fundamentals include exposure triangle (aperture, shutter speed, ISO), focus, and composition. Digital sensors replaced film while preserving optical principles. Post-processing adjusts tone, color, and contrast while sparking debates about manipulation.

Genres include portrait, landscape, documentary, street, and conceptual photography. Masters like Ansel Adams, Dorothea Lange, and Cindy Sherman expanded possibilities. Museums now collect photographs as art. Social media democratized image-making while challenging notions of authorship.
