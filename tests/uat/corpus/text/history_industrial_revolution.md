# The Industrial Revolution

The Industrial Revolution (1760-1840) transformed agrarian societies into industrial economies. Britain led innovations in textile manufacturing, iron production, and steam power. Factories replaced cottage industries.

Steam engines powered locomotives and ships, shrinking travel times. The railway network connected cities and markets. Coal fueled manufacturing but created pollution and dangerous mining conditions.

Social changes were profound. Urbanization crowded workers into industrial cities. Labor movements formed unions demanding better wages and conditions. Child labor was common until reform laws. The revolution created unprecedented wealth while generating inequality and environmental costs.
