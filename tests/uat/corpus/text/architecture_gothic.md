# Gothic Cathedral Architecture

Gothic architecture emerged in 12th-century France and dominated European building for 400 years. Key innovations include pointed arches, ribbed vaults, and flying buttresses that distributed structural loads and allowed thinner walls with larger windows.

Notre-Dame de Paris, Chartres Cathedral, and Cologne Cathedral exemplify the Gothic style. Their soaring naves reach heights of 30-40 meters. Stained glass windows filled interiors with colored light, depicting biblical scenes and saints.

The rose window is a signature Gothic element, featuring intricate stone tracery radiating from a central point. Gargoyles served as decorative water spouts. Construction of a single cathedral could span centuries, with master builders passing techniques to apprentices.
