# Genetics and DNA Sequencing

DNA contains the genetic instructions for all living organisms. The double helix structure discovered by Watson and Crick consists of four nucleotide bases: adenine, thymine, cytosine, and guanine.

Modern sequencing technologies like CRISPR and next-generation sequencing have revolutionized biology. The Human Genome Project mapped the complete human genome in 2003. Today, whole genome sequencing costs under $1000.

Gene editing enables precise modifications to DNA sequences. Applications include treating genetic diseases, developing disease-resistant crops, and synthetic biology. Ethical considerations surround germline editing and genetic privacy as these technologies advance.
