# The Art of Baking Bread

Bread baking combines flour, water, yeast, and salt through fermentation. Yeast consumes sugars and produces carbon dioxide, creating the airy crumb structure. Gluten development through kneading provides elasticity.

Temperature control is crucial. Water should be 105-110°F for yeast activation. Dough rises best at 75-80°F. The Maillard reaction creates golden crusts at temperatures above 300°F.

Different breads require different techniques. Baguettes need steam injection for crusty exteriors. Brioche incorporates butter and eggs for richness. Sourdough uses wild yeast starters developed over days. Proper proofing, scoring, and baking times separate amateur from artisan loaves.
